# Closest research and contribution comparison

Expanded October 19, 2026: 15 studies across historical forecast interpretation, news-based measurement and forecasting, and validation methods. This is a structured comparison, not a systematic review or an exhaustive novelty search. The three closest studies below retain the October 14 methods notes. Added entries distinguish inspected methods from abstract-only screening. Working-paper descriptions must be reconciled with final articles before submission; an accessible working paper does not establish that every final specification is identical.

The publication plan previously attributed the linked 1937–1938 working paper to Mathy and Stekler. Its title page names **Gabriel Mathy and Christian Roatta**. The plan has been corrected. The two GWU working papers request author contact before publication citation; check final versions or resolve that request before submission. No contact has been made.

The potential distinguishing result is **the sensitivity of economic conclusions to forecast interpretation**, supported by source review and a transparent target/horizon contract. That is an inference about a possible contribution, not an established finding. The present evidence does not show that our pipeline improves on these studies or that an explicit-forecast signal outperforms their news measures.

## Comparison rules for writing

The strongest potential contribution is a measured account of how translating an attributable newspaper forecast into a target, horizon and comparator affects the economic conclusion. This remains a project interpretation: the historical sensitivity tables show dependence on scoring choices, while the source audit must establish which choices are defensible.

| Dimension | Closest comparison | Evidence this project needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit and historical scope | Mathy–Stekler and Mathy–Roatta directly evaluate historical outlooks; Bybee and Manela–Moreira already reach deep into historical newspapers | Claim lineage and source context; describe selected newspapers rather than the entire US press |
| Human validation | Shapiro et al. evaluate tone against human ratings; Baker et al. validate uncertainty classification | Forecast validity, field validity and joint scoring usability on our own sampled records; pilot results are separate |
| Meaning of the target | Narrative outlook, sentiment, topic attention and uncertainty are distinct constructs | Quantity, level versus growth, direction, conditionality, attribution and source-supported horizon |
| Incremental information | Balke–Petersen and Barbaglia et al. supply explicit nontext benchmark comparisons | Matched macro-only and press-augmented evaluations; preserve crisis sensitivity and time dependence |
| Historical feasibility | Croushore–Stark motivates vintage discipline; Manela–Moreira's backward reconstruction has a different information set | Freeze features, transformations and labels by availability date; never equate revised-data reconstruction with real-time feasibility |
| Generalization | No retrieved paper establishes our corpus's validity or RNN superiority | Independent future data after freezing choices, with disclosure of prior exposure; existing historical searches remain exploratory |

## Remaining literature work before submission

The 15 entries are a purposeful comparison set, not 15 fully replicated studies. The five abstract-screened entries (Tetlock, García, Baker–Bloom–Davis, Loughran–McDonald, Gentzkow–Kelly–Taddy) still need full-method checks before detailed claims about annotation, timing, baselines or dependence. Reconcile working and final versions for the closest studies. The [Mathy author research page](https://gabrielmathy.weebly.com/research.html), checked October 19, still lists the Roatta project under working papers; do not invent a publication year or final DOI.

Replication status is **not attempted for all 15**. A paper link is not a verified replication package. Before implementing any literature benchmark, inspect its code/data licensing, vocabulary-training dates, vintage treatment and common target support. The comparison does not establish a “first” claim, superiority over published methods, a causal effect of news, or that economic uncertainty measures political polarization.
