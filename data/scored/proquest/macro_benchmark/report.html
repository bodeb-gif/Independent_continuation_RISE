A simpler macro benchmark, selected using earlier data

Annual verified ProQuest claims, 1900–2010. Exploratory continuation after the original model's error audit.

Finding: the simpler benchmark lowers Brier and log loss in both studies and eliminates the earlier forecasts above 90% confidence. The incremental newspaper gain is smaller than in the original comparison. Its core interval includes no gain, and outside 2007–2009 the combined model has slightly worse Brier loss than economy-only. These results still do not establish reliable incremental newspaper skill.

Selection and information timing

Candidate feature sets are six-month production growth alone, or with the three-month unemployment-rate change. The CPI check appends 12-month inflation to both. C is 0.001, 0.01 or 0.1; smaller means stronger regularization. History is expanding or the latest 120 eligible mature months. This gives twelve candidates per study.

Each January, consider the preceding five January refits. Fit each inner model using the production-label vintage available at that inner refit, with the three-month maturity allowance. Forecast its following calendar months. Score only months whose labels are mature and available by the outer January, using that outer vintage. Later revisions known by the outer refit may alter validation labels; final revised evaluation truth never enters selection.

At least 60 training months and all three training classes are required per fit. Select the lowest mean Brier score on identical validation months, requiring at least 24 months from two inner refits. A candidate that cannot fit every eligible fold is unavailable. Ties prefer fewer features, smaller C, then expanding history. With inadequate validation, use the fixed production-only, C=0.001, expanding fallback (plus inflation in the CPI study). Scale within each fit's training data only. No imputation, clipping, class weighting or evaluation-set recalibration is used.

CPI downturn ranking deteriorates substantially: fall AUC falls from 0.936 to 0.213 for economy-only, and from 0.987 to 0.277 with newspapers. The short study remains a weak recession-discrimination benchmark despite avoiding its earlier extreme probability errors. Core fall AUC improves, so the effect is not uniform across histories.

Limits and reproduction

The primary result is incremental newspaper information beyond the selected macro benchmark. Intervals resample fixed forecasts in 2/5/10-year blocks; they do not rerun training or selection. Short CPI intervals have very few blocks and its ten-year interval has only one. They cannot support strong generalization claims. Overlapping target windows, historical revisions, stale quarterly snapshots and changing economic conditions remain limitations.

The candidate family was motivated by previously inspected results. Inner time splits prevent direct future-label leakage within a refit, but do not make the historical evaluation an untouched holdout. Query selection, machine-extracted labels and missing quotations still require human validation. No LOC or old crisis-window claims are included.
