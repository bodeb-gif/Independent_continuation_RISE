How much does verification change the results?

Annual ProQuest economy exports · 1900–2010 · raw sensitivity versus primary machine-verified set

With equal article weights, raw accuracy is 41.7% and verified accuracy is 40.9%. Their gaps against the fixed-direction baseline are -13.5 and -12.7 percentage points. After matching exact publication-month cells, the verified-minus-raw baseline-gap difference is -0.4 points.

These are comparisons of exported datasets, not a causal estimate of verification quality. Raw candidates can be false forecasts. Machine verification is not human validation. Existing query selection and benchmark limitations apply to both sets.

What this first comparison tells us

The exported sets differ in composition: explicitly stated horizon labels rise from 30.0% to 43.1%. The aggregate accuracy result should therefore be read alongside its corresponding baseline and the matched comparisons. The month-matched difference has a five-year-block interval of -1.44 to +0.56 percentage points. This interval crosses zero, so a stable performance difference between these exports is not established.

Year cells match newspaper, publication year, topic, benchmark, resolved horizon basis, and exact horizon in months, requiring at least 10 claims per set. Month cells additionally match publication month and require five claims per set. No cells match on forecast direction or correctness. Article weights start as one divided by the number of scorable claims in the article in each set. Cell means use these weights; both sets then receive the same harmonic-overlap cell mass (2 × raw mass × verified mass / total mass). This changes the target population to shared support. Month cells share identical realized outcomes and baseline values; year cells can still differ in within-year timing. Matched cells and their claim counts are exported for inspection. The unadjusted/matched difference is not a causal decomposition.

Scoring and reproducibility

The explicit raw source adapter uses the same deterministic scorer as verified claims, without inventing a verification field. Only national claims in four supported topics are scored. Horizons must resolve to 1–24 months; vague horizons use the documented 6/12/24-month rules. Flat bands are 2% for industrial production, 5% for stock prices, 1.5% for CPI, and 0.5 percentage points for unemployment. Pre-1919 business claims use the existing NBER proxy and are excluded in a separate sensitivity. Prices versus inflation, output versus growth, unemployment versus employment, and individual assets versus a broad index remain semantic limitations without source quotes. Frozen revised references describe realized outcomes; no print-time economic model is fitted.

Source exports are unchanged; identical label rows are retained. LOC and old crisis-window exports are excluded. Verifier prose and retrospective window annotations do not enter outputs or comparisons. scored_claims.csv.gz uses (source_set, record_id) as a unique source-local address, never as cross-set claim lineage. article_changes.csv pairs only source article IDs and leaves absent-set counts missing. The manifest records source, reference, code hashes and dependency versions; exclusions.csv records one precedence-ordered exclusion reason per unscored row.

Rebuild from the repository root: python src/proquest_robustness.py. Dependencies: requirements-proquest.txt. All tables and figures are stored beside this standalone report.
