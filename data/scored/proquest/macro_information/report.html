Do newspapers add information beyond the economy?

Annual verified ProQuest economy claims, 1900–2010. Exploratory forward-time evaluation with historical macro vintages.

Finding: adding newspapers lowers average core Brier loss, but the block interval includes no gain. The economy-only model itself performs worse than the recent base rate. Adding newspapers beats that base rate on Brier but not log loss, and loses to it on both losses outside 2007–2009. This does not establish reliable incremental forecasting skill.

The source workbooks retain original bytes and hashes. Production snapshots begin November 1962; unemployment starts 1965Q4. CPI has empty earlier columns: its first nonempty snapshot is 1994Q3. These produce first usable forecast origins December 1965 for the core inputs and September 1994 with CPI. Historical snapshots are reconstructed from publications; this archive is not a guarantee of perfect original real-time measurement.

At the first of each month, choose the latest nonempty vintage strictly earlier than issuance. Production's month-end bound conservatively follows that month's release. Quarterly unemployment/CPI snapshots use February/May/August/November 15. Use each snapshot's last observed month and record its age. Changes are calculated within a vintage, so index rebasing cannot create spurious cross-vintage growth. No revised values fill gaps.

Core predictors are production growth over 3/6/12 months, unemployment level and 3/12-month changes, and observation ages. The shorter check adds CPI 3/12-month growth and its age. Quarterly unemployment and CPI can lag information actually published monthly, making this a conservative, incomplete macro benchmark rather than the best possible forecaster.

Outcome, models, and eligibility

Target: the final revised production change from t−1 to t+5; above +2% is rise, below −2% fall, otherwise flat. The starting month's level need not be known at issuance: it is an outcome endpoint, never an input at t. Every January refit instead computes training labels within its latest available production vintage. Endpoint plus three months must precede refit; both endpoints must exist. Historical coverage gaps are excluded and exported in the training-label audit.

All three models use identical eligible training and evaluation months within each study. Eight core macro variables (eleven with CPI) are compared with those same inputs plus four newspaper signals: prior-three-month net direction, common-paper direction change, disagreement, and cross-paper dispersion. Newspaper observations end in t−1. Equal article weights within paper/topic/month and equal paper weights form the panel. Missing inputs exclude a month for every model; no imputation or outcome-based feature selection. Prior panel coverage thresholds remain fixed.

The recent base rate uses the latest 120 eligible mature training months, with one pseudo-observation per class. Both regressions are multinomial ridge logistic models, C=1, with training-only standardization and no tuning. Refit annually on an expanding eligible history; core evaluation starts 1977 with at least 120 training months, CPI evaluation starts 2001 with at least 60. Different training histories mean CPI/core differences do not isolate the contribution of CPI.

Documented implementation deviation: the January 2001 CPI training sample has 48 rise, 20 flat and zero fall labels. Its three-class fit stopped before making predictions. We excluded that year for all three models, leaving 108 common months in 2002–2010. This rule was recorded after the first core results, before any CPI predictions; see implementation_note.json. The original specification is preserved. The core comparison is unchanged.

Calibration

Fixed tenths bins for each class; dot area increases with bin count. Sparse high-probability bins are noisy. No calibration is fitted on evaluation outcomes. Brier sums three squared probability errors (range 0–2, uniform 2/3); log loss is also lower-is-better. Neither accuracy nor calibration is substituted for the primary probability-loss test.

The CPI regressions have high probability losses, both worse than their recent-base-rate comparator. Adding newspapers improves that weak macro fit but does not make it a good probability forecast. High fall AUC is not evidence of calibration: ranking can be good while probabilities are badly wrong. After excluding 2007–2009, the CPI evaluation contains no fall outcomes, so fall AUC/AP are undefined. We retain the preset model rather than tuning against these results.

Interpretation and limits

The primary question is the incremental newspaper gain over the vintage macro model, not its gain over a constant baseline. Paired blocks preserve overlap within blocks, but finite boundaries, few long blocks, overlapping targets and conditioning on fitted models limit uncertainty estimates. The ten-year CPI interval has only one block and is degenerate; it supplies no useful uncertainty estimate. Two/five-year results are also based on few blocks. Secondary comparisons are unadjusted for multiplicity.

These periods have already been explored. Rules were frozen before these fits, not before all historical analysis. Machine-extracted labels, missing quotes, a downturn/recovery query, changing paper coverage and a broad production proxy remain limitations. Human validation remains essential. No speaker identity, verifier prose, retrospective window annotation, LOC claims, or old crisis exports enter the inputs. Results do not establish causation.
