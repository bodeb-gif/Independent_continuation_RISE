## Where the improvement and failures occurred

Business improves from 42.5% to 75.3%, employment from 45.1% to 81.7%; markets are essentially unchanged at 47.4% versus 47.5%. Prices fall from 28.3% to 15.1%. CPI-flat outcomes account for 792/1,130 price claims (70.1%), but only 54 (4.8%) predict flat prices. 617 predict falling prices and 459 rising prices; 52 and 85 are correct, respectively. Flat means an endpoint CPI change within ±1.5%, not zero inflation throughout the horizon. CPI matching is a broad consumer-price proxy; stripped quotes cannot establish that each claim concerns CPI rather than a particular commodity or price.

## Warnings versus optimistic misses

2008 business worsening calls: 4,143/4,233 correct (97.9%). Improvement calls: 6/1,191 correct (0.5%). Nevertheless 1,233 of 5,376 decline outcomes receive an improve/flat call (22.9% missed); 1,146 receive an improve call. Unemployment-rise predictions: 854/900 correct (94.9%); unemployment-fall predictions: 0/139 correct. Mechanically calling every business horizon a decline would score 97.5% in this cohort, and every unemployment horizon a rise would score 94.6%. These are retrospective state-direction comparisons, not a strategy selected before 2008 or evidence that the recession was anticipated.

## Chronology: foresight and reversal

In 2007 business accuracy rises from 18.6% in Q1 and 9.2% in Q2 to 48.8% in Q3 and 66.7% in Q4. Worsening-call shares rise from 55.6%/59.0% in Q1/Q2 to 73.8%/72.8% in Q3/Q4. The sample already contains many worsening calls before the crisis; the 2006 share is 64.6%, with only 3/354 correct. Thus the rise in correct warnings combines more pessimistic calls with a change in which direction became correct. NBER later dated the peak to December 2007, announced December 1, 2008; all 2008 publication months are retrospectively recession months in USREC. Lehman filed September 15, 2008. Before that date the business warning share is 73.3%, afterward 81.9%; business accuracy is 71.6% versus 80.8%. Unequal date windows and shifting composition prevent interpreting this as a causal response to Lehman. Overall quarterly accuracy falls sharply from 68.4% in 2008Q4 to 31.0% in 2009Q1.

## The large misses move into 2009

Business worsening calls in 2009: 57/2,388 correct (2.4%); 2,331 false alarms comprise 1,567 growth and 764 flat outcomes. Improvement calls: 1,777/2,213 correct (80.3%). In 2009Q1, business accuracy is 14.5%, with 67.9% calling worsening but only 6.0% of horizon outcomes declining. Across all 2009, output-growth endpoints account for 72.8% of business claims. Market worsening calls: 2/664 correct; improvement calls: 508/530 correct (95.8%). Almost all market endpoints rise (1,192/1,250), yet 53.1% of calls still predict worsening. Employment accuracy also drops to 32.6%: 960/1,777 outcomes are flat under the ±0.5-point unemployment rule, and only 16 calls predict flat. These labels suggest difficulty adjusting expectations around turning points, but do not prove that the same forecasters persisted in the same view; this is an aggregate sample, not a speaker panel.

## Composition and horizon checks

The same five publisher families represented in 2007, 2008 and 2009 score 41.8%, 64.3%, and 36.8%. Boston Globe and Los Angeles Times have no scored 2007 claims in these exports, so full-year publisher coverage differs. Shared paper/topic/benchmark/horizon-basis/exact-horizon cells with ten claims on each side score 44.2% in 2007 versus 63.9% in 2008 (42 cells; +19.7 points), and 66.8% in 2008 versus 37.1% in 2009 (86 cells; -29.6 points). Neither comparison controls publication month or economic state; they describe similar forecast composition, not causal differences. Claim-grounded accuracy is 30.5%, 58.4%, 37.6%; explicitly stated horizons score 29.0%, 57.7%, 38.0%. 2008 default-horizon claims score 69.5%, so defaults amplify the peak but do not create it.

## Scoring examples and limits

The endpoint examples use frozen revised references. Business uses ±2% industrial production; markets ±5% monthly-average Shiller prices; prices ±1.5% CPI; employment ±0.5 percentage point unemployment. January 2008 to January 2009 output falls 13.4%, stock prices fall 37.2%, CPI changes only +0.03%, and unemployment rises 2.8 points. December 2008 stock prices to December 2009 rise 26.5%, so a bearish forecast near the crash may fail at its later endpoint. A directional hit does not measure magnitude, precise timing, recession-onset prediction, or tail-risk anticipation. The original query contains recession/downturn/depression/recovery/slump near prediction language; it is a selected corpus, not an unbiased panel of all press forecasts. High crisis-era claim counts do not establish more press attention without article denominators. Machine verification is not human validation; stripped quotes prevent causal narrative reconstruction, semantic rechecking, and definitive deduplication. Rows sharing an article and monthly benchmark outcome are not independent trials.
