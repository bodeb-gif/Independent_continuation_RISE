Do newspaper forecasts add economic information?

Exploratory annual-refit evaluation · 1950–2010 · six-month industrial-production direction

Adding four newspaper signals changes the multiclass Brier score from 0.6397 to 0.5739. The improvement is +0.0658 (positive is better), with a five-year-block interval of +0.0214 to +0.1144. The press model improves on the base rate in 10 of 13 five-year evaluation blocks.

This tests future economic direction, rather than whether individual claims are correct. A base-rate comparison does not establish added value beyond an economic model using historically available macro data. All results are exploratory: this historical corpus has already informed earlier analyses.

1. A reusable monthly newspaper panel

The panel contains 182,817 readable national claims across four supported topics, including claims whose horizons or eventual outcomes cannot be scored. The existing verified export has 274,070 claims in total. Every article receives equal weight within its newspaper/topic/month; observed newspapers then receive equal weight. Identical stripped rows are retained. Empty newspaper-months have zero coverage and missing signal values, not artificial neutral forecasts.

The paper panel includes positive, negative and flat shares, net direction, disagreement, one-month changes, and claim/article coverage. Positive means improving business/markets/employment; for prices it means rising prices, not good news. Disagreement is normalized three-category Gini impurity. Cross-paper dispersion is the population standard deviation of paper net directions. The model uses business forecasts only. Counts describe these exports, never overall press attention.

2. Outcome and information timing

A forecast is issued at the start of month t, using completed print months only. Its target is the change in monthly-average industrial production from t−1 to t+5: six monthly-index intervals. Changes above +2% are rises, below −2% are falls, and the rest are flat, including the threshold boundaries. This is a broad business proxy, not literal recession-onset prediction. The starting industrial-production value need not have been released at issuance; it is used only for retrospective outcome scoring.

Training outcomes begin with forecast origin February 1919. Each January from 1950 through 2010, models refit on an expanding history, retaining only labels whose endpoint plus a three-month release allowance is strictly before that January. The fitted model then predicts the next 12 months as newspaper context arrives. No tuning uses evaluation outcomes. The economic series is a revised snapshot: the release allowance prevents obvious overlap, but cannot establish genuine historical vintage availability. This is a retrospective experiment using publication-dated press features.

Historical base rate: earlier mature class frequencies with one pseudo-observation per class. Coverage only: multinomial ridge logistic regression using four feature-missingness flags. Base rate + press: multinomial ridge logistic regression with fitted class intercepts, the four press signals and the same missingness flags. The regression intercepts are fitted jointly with predictors rather than fixed to the smoothed base rate. Both regressions use C=1 with no parameter search; imputation medians and scaling are fitted on training months only.

The four signals are prior-three-month net direction, its change versus the preceding three months among common papers, prior-three-month disagreement, and cross-paper dispersion. Signal windows require at least two observed months, two newspapers, and five articles. Direction changes require two common papers and adequate coverage in both windows. All outcome months remain in the primary evaluation; missing signals are imputed with training medians and flagged. Counts and newspaper identities are not predictors.

There are 732 evaluation months, one forecast per month, not a separate independent outcome per claim. Brier score sums squared probability errors across three classes (0–2, lower is better); log loss is also lower-is-better. Accuracy is the argmax class hit rate; balanced accuracy averages recalls over classes present in the evaluation subset. Fall AUC/AP use the predicted probability of a greater-than-2% fall.

Intervals jointly resample forecast-year blocks across all models, preserving within-block overlapping targets. Two-, five-, and ten-year lengths, 2,000 draws each, are exported. They condition on fitted forecasts and describe historical variation; they are not fresh-holdout confirmation or complete uncertainty about model fitting, extraction errors, and query selection. Secondary comparisons are not adjusted for multiple testing.

The selected downturn/recovery query, changing publication mix, machine label errors, stripped quotations, and broad production proxy limit interpretation. Equal-paper weights and common-paper changes reduce some composition effects but do not reconstruct missing articles. Printed content is the intended information source, but retrospective machine extraction can itself carry hindsight errors. No macro values, economic outcomes, recession labels, verifier prose, speaker identities, or retrospective annotations are model features. LOC and old crisis-window exports are excluded.

Reproduce and inspect

python src/proquest_information.py uses the saved evaluation_spec.json. Dependencies are in requirements-proquest-information.txt. The output folder includes article and newspaper/topic/month panels, national monthly panels, all forecast features and outcomes, annual refit audits, preprocessing, model coefficients, monthly probabilities, losses, calibration, metrics and stability tables. Source/reference/code/specification hashes and versions are in summary.json. All source exports remain unchanged.
