METHODS PAPER: NOVELTY AND REALISTIC JOURNAL TARGETS
Assessment date: 25 October 2026

VERDICT

A publishable applied measurement paper is plausible after targeted revision.
The present evidence does not establish a new extraction architecture, a new
statistical estimator, or a generally superior low-cost model. The current
manuscript is borderline as a methods contribution because its broad lessons
are already well established. The defensible contribution is a particular
empirical audit of how explicit historical economic forecasts become scored
measurements, with a reusable procedure and source-linked error evidence.

Recommended first target: Journal of Computational Social Science (JCSS),
given its subject fit, the existing submission package, and a no-APC route.
PLOS ONE is a plausible alternative if funding is available and the paper is
framed as rigorous original empirical research. Quality & Quantity is a
credible methods-focused alternative after making the generalizable procedure
and measurement logic more explicit. These are fit judgments, not estimated
acceptance probabilities. None guarantees referees' reports within 60 days.

CLOSEST WORK AND CONSEQUENCES

1. Pangakis, Wolken and Fasching (2023), Automated Annotation with Generative
   AI Requires Validation. The arXiv paper tests 27 tasks across 11 datasets
   and argues for task-specific human validation. Therefore inexpensive LLM
   annotation plus a human audit is not a sufficient novelty claim.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00176

2. Egami, Hinck, Stewart and Wei (NeurIPS 2023), Using Imperfect Surrogates
   for Downstream Inference: Design-based Supervised Learning for Social
   Science Applications of Large Language Models. High label accuracy can
   coexist with biased inference; the paper supplies a correction estimator
   using a probability-sampled human reference. Our saved correction belongs
   in this established literature; do not present it as an invented estimator
   or claim all their guarantees automatically apply to our ratio estimands.
   https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/d862f7f5445255090de13b825b880d59-Abstract-Conference.html

3. Ashwin, Chhabra and Rao, Using Large Language Models for Qualitative
   Analysis can Introduce Serious Bias. Sociological Methods & Research,
   online 2025, volume 55(3), 795-839 (2026). Their interview application
   demonstrates nonrandom annotation errors and misleading inference.
   Thus finding errors or downstream sensitivity alone is not conceptually new.
   https://doi.org/10.1177/00491241251338246
   Author-institution publication record:
   https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/using-large-language-models-for-qualitative-analysis-can-introduc-2/

4. Ludwig, Mullainathan and Rambachan (2026), Large Language Models: An
   Applied Econometric Framework, Annual Review of Economics 18:283-316.
   Distinguishes prediction from measurement for estimation; explains
   validation-based correction and training leakage. Our paper needs to
   distinguish retrospective source interpretation from historically feasible
   forecasting. A human-corrected historical application can contribute
   evidence, but the correction rationale is established.
   https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-economics-120925-105620

5. Bao (30 July 2026), Beyond the Conventional Pipeline: Large Language
   Models for Social Science Measurement, Chinese Political Science Review.
   Explicitly separates constructing a measure from downstream inference and
   discusses validation, sensitivity and error propagation. This is a close
   conceptual predecessor to an 'extraction is not measurement' framing.
   Our contribution must be an operational audit and empirical results.
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41111-026-00351-4

6. Hansen (March 2026), Validating Large Language Model Annotations, FEDS
   2026-020, WORKING PAPER. Proposes reconstruction-based validation and
   applies it to economic news. A general validation framework for LLM-coded
   news is already occupied territory. Our human-reviewed, claim-level
   target/horizon and scoring analysis remains a different application.
   https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/validating-large-language-model-annotations.htm

7. Mathy and Stekler (2017), Expectations and Forecasting during the Great
   Depression, and the related Mathy-Roatta 1937-1938 project are direct
   predecessors for evaluating historical press forecasts. The repository's
   existing literature review inspected their working-paper methods. Neither
   historical newspapers nor retrospective qualitative forecast evaluation is
   new. Source-specific target, scope and horizon auditing at claim level is
   the relevant distinction, subject to better source validation.
   https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0164070416301446
   Detailed existing comparison: paper/references/literature.md at the fixed
   source commit above.

Additional recent caution: Nagel, Tseng and Xiu (2026), Completions, Not
Convictions: Can LLM Probabilities Proxy for Human Beliefs? WORKING PAPER,
SSRN 7265538. Its abstract reports altered economic conclusions when LLM
probabilities substitute for human beliefs. This studies elicited probabilities,
not our extraction of attributed statements, but further rules out claiming
that model-derived quantities changing conclusions is itself a new discovery.
Screened from primary SSRN indexed abstract; full text not inspected here.
https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7265538

WHAT THE PROJECT CAN ACTUALLY ADD

Suggested title:
From extracted forecasts to valid economic measurements:
A human audit of historical newspaper predictions

Suggested contribution statement:
We evaluate the separate stages required to turn an extracted forecast into
an economically interpretable scored observation: forecast inclusion, target
and scope eligibility, direction and horizon interpretation, and outcome
matching. A paired human audit measures what an additional verification pass
improves, which valid observations it removes, and which measurement problems
remain. We trace the consequences for benchmark comparisons using existing
human labels and explicitly stated correction assumptions.

Evidence already available:

- In the 250-claim historical audit, the extra verifier retained 214 claims.
  Forecast-inclusion precision improved from 70.8% to 77.6% against reviewer 1
  and 68.4% to 76.6% against reviewer 2. It removed 25/29 reviewer-invalid
  candidates but also 11/7 reviewer-valid forecasts. These are paired
  candidate-level results, not source-document extraction recall.
- Exact-row linkage to the saved reviewer-1 human rescore gives 104 eligible
  claims among the 214 retained (48.6%), compared with 110 among all 250
  before the additional pass. Six eligible claims were removed. The 77.6%
  inclusion result and the 48.6% benchmark-eligibility result measure different
  properties. Eligibility is not full joint field accuracy or source recall.
- For the same 9,260 employment claims, scoring against unemployment versus
  payrolls changes the baseline-relative point estimate from positive to
  negative. This demonstrates target sensitivity, not a proven statistical
  reversal or proof that either target is the source-correct interpretation.
- The saved reviewer-1 correction changes a claim-weighted machine-only gap
  of -12.81 percentage points to -1.84 points, with a 95% interval of
  [-16.56, 12.88]. Report alongside the human-only estimator (-10.91 points,
  interval [-20.79, -1.03]), not instead of it. The disagreement makes the
  small audit's uncertainty important; it does not establish that forecasts
  outperform the baseline or that the true gap is zero.

The manuscript itself acknowledges that filtering leaves unchanged labels
unchanged on identical survivors. That is true by construction and cannot be
the principal empirical discovery. The measured changes in population,
eligibility, errors, and resulting estimates are the informative content.

PRIORITY REVISIONS

1. Complete the chain from audit to estimand with BOTH existing reviewers.
   The saved main correction specification currently supplies reviewer-1
   results; the earlier local work adds only a small reviewer-2 labor subset,
   not a full reviewer-2 correction. Join exact audit IDs, show before/after
   inclusion and eligibility, and report all prespecified weighting/horizon
   variants. Retain human disagreement and the partial machine-label exposure
   of reviewer 1. Do not relabel that review as fully blind or a gold standard.
   Sampling intervals condition on a fixed frame and human labels; they omit
   reviewer error, macroeconomic generalization and target ambiguity.

2. Make the audit a reusable procedure, with a codebook, deterministic target
   and scope rules, an abstention category for unresolvable claims, scoring
   rules, inclusion probabilities and exact lineage. Show which decisions a
   researcher can make using it. Existing compatible human horizon groups
   sometimes preserve machine numeric horizons and otherwise default vague
   horizons; these are not exact independently source-confirmed endpoints.

3. For a stronger methods claim, evaluate a concrete improvement against the
   existing extra-filter baseline: for example targeted correction of scope,
   quantity and horizon with abstention. Freeze the procedure before testing
   on an untouched audit sample. Evaluate joint scoreability, valid-claim loss,
   reviewer agreement, downstream error and cost. Report failures too. The
   current 300 reviewed cases can develop this procedure but cannot also be
   presented as its untouched test set. A useful case study need not claim a
   new improvement; a claimed new method needs evidence of utility.

4. Strengthen transfer evidence economically. A separate source, newspaper,
   topic or era can help, but any previously inspected slice is exploratory.
   The 50 modern reviews alone do not establish broad generalization. Choose
   new audit size using precision/power and clustering, not an arbitrary
   threshold. No new paid API or VM extraction is authorized by this report.

5. Update the related-work section with 2023-2026 predecessors, finish author
   and declaration fields, and provide an accessible reproducibility package
   that respects ProQuest permissions. Keep restricted text in the authorized
   VM; do not promise unrestricted raw text. Public-domain worked examples can
   demonstrate the procedure without serving as a substitute validation set.

Steps 1, 2 and 5 are the shortest credible submission route with existing
assets. Steps 3 and 4 offer the larger improvement in methodological novelty,
but require new validation work. None guarantees acceptance.

DEADLINE AND TWO-PAPER STRATEGY

If 'response in under two months' includes editorial rejection, the advertised
medians make several targets plausible. If it means substantive external
referee reports, the verified data do not establish that any target reliably
meets the requirement. If it means acceptance, there is no defensible promise.
Timings start at submission, not at the beginning of manuscript revision.
Use sequential submissions; a fast rejection can consume less time but is
not evidence of a better publication route. No editor was contacted.

Keep methods-paper claims centered on measurement reliability and economic
consequences of operationalization. Reserve the proposed labor-warning and
cross-topic findings for the substantive paper, with independent validation
and full search/uncertainty disclosure. Shared data are compatible with two
papers when the primary questions and contributions differ and related work
is disclosed. A renamed set of the same main results would not justify a
second publication.
